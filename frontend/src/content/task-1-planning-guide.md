# IELTS Academic Task 1: Five-Minute Planning Guide

Task 1 is a short report of at least 150 words. Your plan should therefore be short too.

Use four notes:

1. **Introduction note** — What does the visual show?
2. **Overview** — What are the most important overall features?
3. **Detail paragraph 1** — Which related evidence belongs together?
4. **Detail paragraph 2** — What remaining evidence or contrast belongs together?

Do not plan a conclusion. Academic Task 1 normally needs an introduction, an overview, and two detail paragraphs.

## A five-minute routine

- **0:00–0:45 — Read:** identify the subject, units, dates, places, or stages.
- **0:45–1:45 — Find the overview:** choose two or three features that summarize the whole visual.
- **1:45–3:15 — Form detail group 1:** put related facts, categories, areas, or stages together.
- **3:15–4:30 — Form detail group 2:** cover the remaining important evidence and comparisons.
- **4:30–5:00 — Check:** make sure the overview is clear, both detail groups have evidence, and nothing important is omitted.

Use fragments and abbreviations. This is an idea-and-organization exercise, not a grammar exercise.

## Line graphs

**Overview:** main rises, falls, peaks, low points, or stable trends.

**Details:** group lines with similar movements. Use selected starting, ending, peak, or crossing values rather than every year.

## Bar charts

**Overview:** highest and lowest categories, the largest gap, and any obvious similarity.

**Details:** group similar or contrasting categories. Do not describe every bar from left to right.

## Tables

**Overview:** largest and smallest figures, repeated patterns, and clear outliers.

**Details:** compare meaningful rows or columns. Select representative figures instead of reporting every cell.

## Pie charts

**Overview:** dominant and smallest shares. When years are compared, note the largest increases, decreases, or stable shares.

**Details:** group the major shares in one paragraph and smaller or changing shares in the other.

## Maps and plans

**Overview:** the largest developments and anything that stayed unchanged.

**Details:** group changes by area or purpose, such as transport in one paragraph and buildings or facilities in the other.

## Processes, systems, and lifecycles

**Overview:** starting point, final result, broad phases, and whether the sequence is linear or cyclical.

**Details:** divide the sequence into early and later stages. Describe only what the diagram shows.

## Mixed visuals

**Overview:** one main observation from each visual and any important relationship between them.

**Details:** give every visual a clear role. One paragraph may cover each visual, or both paragraphs may compare related evidence across them.

## Final check

- Did I identify the visual accurately?
- Does the overview summarize rather than list details?
- Do both detail paragraphs have a clear organizing idea?
- Did I select evidence instead of copying everything?
- Did I avoid adding opinions, causes, or predictions not shown?
