# IELTS Academic Writing Task 1: Guide by Question Type

A strong IELTS Academic Task 1 response usually contains four paragraphs:

- **Introduction** — paraphrase what the visual shows.
- **Overview** — summarize the two or three most important features.
- **Detail paragraph 1** — describe one meaningful group of evidence.
- **Detail paragraph 2** — describe a second meaningful group or contrast.

Do not add a separate conclusion.

## Line Graphs

### What to identify

- Overall upward or downward trends
- Stable or fluctuating lines
- Highest and lowest lines
- Peaks and low points
- Crossovers
- Changes in ranking
- Similar or contrasting movements

Do not report every year.

### Introduction

Include what was measured, the groups being compared, the time period, and the unit when necessary.

**Template**

> The line graph compares changes in [measure] among [groups] between [year] and [year].

### Overview

Mention two or three major features: which lines rose or fell, which remained highest or lowest, whether one line overtook another, which category changed most, or whether the lines converged or moved further apart.

**Template**

> Overall, [main trend 1], whereas [main contrast]. In addition, [highest, lowest, crossover, or largest change].

### Detail paragraph 1

Group lines with similar trends, higher values, or another clear relationship. Include selected starting, ending, peak, or crossover figures.

**Template**

> At the beginning of the period, [A] stood at [figure], compared with [figure] for [B]. Thereafter, both figures [describe movement], although [key difference].

### Detail paragraph 2

Describe the contrasting or remaining lines.

**Template**

> By contrast, [C] [movement], rising/falling from [figure] to [figure]. Meanwhile, [D] [remaining pattern].

### Planning example

- Overview: A and B rose; C declined. A highest initially; B highest finally.
- Detail 1: A 20→45, B 15→50, B overtook A in 2018.
- Detail 2: C 40→18, D stable around 10.

## Bar Charts

### What to identify

- Highest and lowest categories
- Major gaps
- Similar values
- Clusters of high or low figures
- Increases and decreases when years are shown
- Categories that remained stable

Do not describe each bar in order without making comparisons.

### Introduction

Include the measure, categories, location or time, and units.

**Template**

> The bar chart compares [measure] across [categories] in [place/year].

### Overview

Mention the dominant category, the smallest category, the largest gap, the most significant change if multiple years are shown, and any obvious similarity.

**Template**

> Overall, [category] recorded the highest figure, while [category] was the lowest. The most notable feature was [main contrast or change].

### Detail paragraph 1

Group larger categories, increasing categories, or categories with similar results.

**Template**

> [A] recorded the highest figure, at [number], followed by [B] at [number]. By comparison, [C] was noticeably lower.

### Detail paragraph 2

Group smaller categories, declining categories, or remaining contrasts.

**Template**

> Turning to the remaining categories, [D] stood at [number], whereas [E] accounted for only [number].

### Planning example

- Overview: cars highest; cycling lowest; rail increased most.
- Detail 1: cars 55→48, rail 15→28.
- Detail 2: bus 18→12, walking 8→9, cycling 4→3.

## Tables

### What to identify

- Highest and lowest values
- Repeated patterns across rows or columns
- Major differences
- Outliers
- Similar figures
- Changes over time

Do not report every cell.

### Introduction

Include what the table shows, groups, dates or places, and units.

**Template**

> The table presents information about [measure] for [groups] in [place/time].

### Overview

Mention the largest category overall, the smallest category overall, repeated patterns, and one important exception or outlier.

**Template**

> Overall, [category] accounted for the largest figures in most groups, while [category] was generally the smallest. The main exception was [group].

### Detail paragraph 1

Compare one meaningful set of rows or columns.

**Template**

> Regarding [category], [group] recorded the highest figure, at [number], followed by [group] at [number].

### Detail paragraph 2

Compare the remaining categories or groups.

**Template**

> A similar pattern can be seen in [groups]. However, [group] differed because [comparison].

### Planning example

- Overview: housing largest in all countries; leisure smallest except Canada; Japan highest for food.
- Detail 1: housing — US 42, UK 38, Canada 35, Japan 28.
- Detail 2: food and leisure — Japan food 30; Canada leisure 20.

## Pie Charts

### What to identify

For one pie chart, look for:

- Largest share
- Smallest share
- Categories forming the majority
- Similar proportions

For multiple pie charts, also look for:

- Largest increase
- Largest decrease
- Stable categories
- Changes in ranking
- Categories gaining or losing importance

### Introduction

Include what the proportions represent, the location, time, and units.

**Template**

> The pie charts compare the proportions of [categories] in [place] in [years].

### Overview

Mention the dominant category, the smallest category, the largest increase or decrease, and whether the distribution became more or less balanced.

**Template**

> Overall, [category] was the largest share initially, but [category] became dominant later. The most significant changes were the rise in [category] and the decline in [category].

### Detail paragraph 1

Group dominant shares, increasing categories, or related categories.

**Template**

> In [year], [category] accounted for [figure], while [category] represented [figure]. By [later year], these figures had changed to [figures].

### Detail paragraph 2

Group declining shares, smaller categories, or stable categories.

**Template**

> In contrast, the proportion of [category] fell from [figure] to [figure], whereas [category] remained relatively stable.

### Planning example

- Overview: coal largest in 2000; solar largest in 2025; coal fell sharply; solar rose most.
- Detail 1: solar 8→35, wind 10→22.
- Detail 2: coal 45→20, oil 25→13, gas stable around 12.

Do not automatically describe one pie chart in each paragraph. Group categories by change or importance when possible.

## Maps and Plans

### What to identify

- Largest developments
- Buildings or facilities added
- Features removed or replaced
- Areas that expanded
- Land-use changes
- Transport changes
- Features that remained unchanged

### Introduction

Include the location, what the maps compare, and the dates.

**Template**

> The maps show how [place] changed between [year] and [year].

### Overview

Mention the general type of transformation, the largest development, and one important unchanged feature when relevant.

**Template**

> Overall, the area became more [residential/commercial/urbanised/accessible], mainly due to [major development]. However, [feature] remained unchanged.

### Detail paragraph 1

Group changes by geographical area, type of development, or function.

**Template**

> In the northern part of the area, [feature] was demolished and replaced by [new feature]. A new [facility] was also constructed nearby.

### Detail paragraph 2

Describe the second area, remaining developments, and unchanged features.

**Template**

> Meanwhile, in the southern section, [change]. By contrast, [feature] remained in its original location.

### Useful location language

- To the north of
- In the south-eastern corner
- Alongside
- Opposite
- Adjacent to
- In the centre of
- On either side of

### Planning example

- Overview: village became more residential and accessible; farmland replaced by housing; church unchanged.
- Detail 1: north/east — farm removed, housing estate and road added.
- Detail 2: south/west — shops expanded, bridge added, church unchanged.

## Processes and Manufacturing Diagrams

### What to identify

- Starting material or input
- Final product
- Number of major stages
- Where the process divides or combines
- Whether the process is linear or cyclical
- Whether stages are natural, mechanical, or both

### Introduction

State what is being produced or completed.

**Template**

> The diagram illustrates how [product] is produced.

### Overview

Mention whether the process is linear or cyclical, the number of major stages, the starting point, and the final result.

**Template**

> Overall, this is a linear process consisting of [number] main stages, beginning with [starting point] and ending with [final result].

### Detail paragraph 1

Describe the early stages in sequence.

**Template**

> Initially, [material] is [passive verb]. It is then [next action], after which [next stage].

### Detail paragraph 2

Describe the later stages.

**Template**

> In the subsequent stage, [material] undergoes [process]. Finally, it is [final action] to produce [result].

### Useful passive verbs

- Is collected
- Is transported
- Is heated
- Is mixed
- Is filtered
- Is stored
- Is packaged

### Planning example

- Overview: linear, seven stages, used paper to new paper rolls.
- Detail 1: collect → sort → mix with water → create pulp.
- Detail 2: clean → press → dry → roll.

## Natural Processes and Lifecycles

### What to identify

- First stage
- Mature or final stage
- Major physical changes
- Habitat changes
- Reproduction
- Whether the cycle repeats

### Introduction

State the lifecycle or natural process shown.

**Template**

> The diagram illustrates the lifecycle of [organism].

### Overview

Mention whether the process is cyclical, the beginning and mature stage, and the broad phases.

**Template**

> Overall, the lifecycle is cyclical and consists of [number] broad stages, beginning with [stage] and culminating in [stage], after which the cycle repeats.

### Detail paragraph 1

Describe early development.

**Template**

> During the initial stages, [organism] develops from [stage] into [stage], before [next transformation].

### Detail paragraph 2

Describe maturation and repetition.

**Template**

> As it matures, [later changes]. The adult then [reproductive stage], causing the cycle to begin again.

### Planning example

- Overview: cyclical; eggs to adult frog; aquatic early stages, land-based adult stage.
- Detail 1: eggs → tadpoles → back legs → front legs.
- Detail 2: tail shortens → young frog → adult → eggs.

## Mixed Visuals

### What to identify

For each visual, identify its main message, its most important figures, and how it relates to the other visual.

Possible relationships include:

- Totals and percentage breakdowns
- Production and consumption
- Population and resource use
- Trends and category composition
- Volume and average value

Do not invent causal relationships.

### Introduction

Mention both visuals.

**Template**

> The [first visual] compares [information], while the [second visual] provides data on [information].

### Overview

Mention the main feature from each visual and the strongest relationship between them when clearly shown.

**Template**

> Overall, [main feature from visual 1]. Meanwhile, [main feature from visual 2], with [important relationship].

### Detail paragraph 1

Cover the first visual or one related topic across both visuals.

**Template**

> In terms of [first measure], [comparison with figures].

### Detail paragraph 2

Cover the second visual or a second related comparison across both visuals.

**Template**

> Turning to [second measure], [comparison with figures].

### Planning example

- Overview: France had the most visitors; US visitors spent the most per person.
- Detail 1: visitor totals — France 12m, Spain 10m, US 7m, Japan 5m.
- Detail 2: spending — US $1,500, Japan $1,200, France $800.

## Fast Grouping Rules

### Time-based charts

- Similar trends
- Increases versus decreases
- Higher versus lower lines
- Early versus later periods, only when the pattern clearly changes

### Static charts

- High versus low categories
- Similar values
- Largest contrasts
- Related demographic or geographical groups

### Maps

- Geographical area
- Type of change
- Changed versus unchanged features

### Processes

- Early versus later stages
- Preparation versus production
- Natural versus human-controlled stages

### Mixed visuals

- One paragraph per visual
- One topic across both visuals
- High-performing versus low-performing categories across both visuals

Choose the grouping that creates the clearest comparisons.
